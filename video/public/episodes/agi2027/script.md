[beat:cold-open]

Five percent.

That is the probability — according to people betting real money — that OpenAI announces it has built artificial general intelligence before the year twenty-twenty-seven.

Five. You can buy "yes" for about a nickel.

[beat:title]

<!-- title card: THE MARKET SAYS · pixel wordmark · ep 02 -->

[beat:setup]

Here's the part nobody tells you. On this market, "OpenAI declares AGI" was never a science question. It was a *contract* question. For years, the moment OpenAI's board declared it had reached AGI, that declaration would sever Microsoft's access to the good stuff. Declaring AGI wasn't a press release. It was a nuclear option worth tens of billions of dollars.

So when this market says five percent, it isn't rating the robots. It's rating a boardroom.

[beat:the-tape]

Here's the tape.

<!-- chart draws on from market open (Aug 2025); cursor + odds counter -->

It opened last August around twenty percent, and by late September the crowd had talked itself up to twenty-four. Peak faith. "This is the year."

[beat:move-panel]

<!-- annotation: 2025-10-28 Microsoft restructuring; expert panel verifies AGI -->

Then reality filed some paperwork. Through October, OpenAI restructured, and the power to declare AGI was taken away from OpenAI's board and handed to an independent panel of experts. Harder to declare, harder to fake. The number bled from twenty-four to eleven.

[beat:move-clause]

<!-- annotation: 2026-04-27 the AGI clause dies -->

And in April, the twist: they killed the clause entirely. Microsoft keeps its rights regardless of what OpenAI builds. Declaring AGI now triggers… nothing. The market convulsed — eighteen, ten, eighteen, ten — traders realizing the big red button had been quietly unplugged.

[beat:meme-react]

<!-- meme_intent: deadpan / unbothered — the button does nothing now -->

[beat:zoom]

<!-- x-domain zooms into Apr 8 – May 2, 2026 (the hourly close-up window) -->

Zoom into those two April weeks and you can watch a market argue with itself in real time — then give up. Because once "declaring AGI" costs nothing and pays nothing, why would anyone rush to do it?

[beat:so-what]

Which leaves us at five percent. Sam Altman now says AGI is "not a super useful term," which is a convenient thing to decide right after the term stopped being worth money.

The machines might still get there. The *market* is just no longer paying for the announcement.
