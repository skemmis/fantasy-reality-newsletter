[beat:cold-open]

Fifty-one percent.

That is the probability — according to people betting actual money — that the Federal Reserve raises interest rates this year.

Raises. As in, up.

[beat:title]

[beat:setup]

This is a prediction market, on a site called Kalshi. Traders buy yes or no on a question — in this case, "will the Fed hike by December thirty-first" — and the price becomes the probability. It's like polling, except everyone who's wrong loses money, which does wonders for honesty.

One point two million dollars of volume has settled on: coin flip.

For reference, the same crowd puts a rate *cut* this year at twenty-five percent. So the smart money — or at least the money — thinks the next move is twice as likely to be up as down. Every economist on television, meanwhile, is still saying "cuts are coming." Someone here is very wrong, and I, personally, cannot wait.

And then it just… kept going. Which brings us to today: fifty-one. A number whose entire personality is "no idea, but armed."

[beat:so-what]

So what do you do with a coin flip? Nothing. That's the point. A prediction market isn't here to tell you the future — it's here to tell you what the future costs. And right now, certainty about the Fed costs more than it has all year.

The Federal Reserve's whole job is to be boring. The market is charging even odds that they fail.
